# Job Plan: Offer by November

Goal: interview-ready by early November and an offer by 30 November. This plan runs alongside
the 30-day fundamentals sprint and turns it toward interviews.

Hiring loops usually take 3–6 weeks from first contact to offer. So applications start in
**week 1**, not after you feel ready. Interviewing is the fastest way to find your gaps.

## Daily routine (weekdays, about 3 h)

- 60 min coding (DSA): 2 problems, timed at 25 min each, then study the best solution and redo it from memory.
- 75 min system design: the sprint day for today, or one interview question once the sprint ends.
- 20 min behavioral or low-level design (LLD): rehearse one story out loud, or sketch one LLD problem.
- 15 min job pipeline: applications, referral asks, recruiter replies, scheduling.

Weekends: one full mock (45 min system design plus 45 min coding), a weekly retro, and catch-up.

## STAR stories to prepare (Tech Lead set)

1. A technical decision you drove that others disagreed with, and how you reached alignment.
2. A production incident you led: detection, mitigation, postmortem, and what changed afterwards.
3. A project that slipped or failed, and what you learned.
4. Mentoring someone into a stronger engineer.
5. Influencing without authority across teams.
6. Cutting scope or paying down tech debt with a business justification.
7. A design you got wrong and how you corrected it.
8. Delivering under ambiguity: turning a vague goal into a plan.

## Ground rules

- Apply before you feel ready. Referrals first: they convert several times better than cold applications.
- Track every application in the site's Job Hunt tab, and follow up after 5 working days.
- After each real interview, write down every question within an hour and drill the weak spots.
- Don't skip sleep for one more problem. Consistency over 7 weeks beats a few all-nighters.
