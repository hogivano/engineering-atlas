# Data-layer incidents and engineering write-ups

A list of real, publicly documented production incidents and detailed engineering write-ups covering databases, storage, replication, caching, data corruption, backups, migrations and search/indexing. Every source is primary: the company's own postmortem, status page or engineering blog, an official report, or a Jepsen / peer-reviewed analysis. Each URL was opened on 2026-10-17 to check the details below. Entries are in chronological order.

Problem-shape vocabulary: contention, duplicates, ordering, fan-out, hot spots, overload, partial failure, multi-step consistency, finding things fast, trust but verify.

## 2011–2016

- **AWS, 2011-04: EBS re-mirroring storm in US-East** — A network change sent primary traffic onto the low-capacity backup network, cutting many EBS nodes off from their replicas. When connectivity returned, the nodes all tried to re-mirror at once. That used up the cluster's free capacity, and the control plane became starved. About 13% of volumes in one AZ got stuck. 0.07% of volumes could not be restored to a consistent state. Single-AZ RDS saw up to 45% of instances with stuck I/O. The event lasted about 3 days (Apr 21–24). Mechanism: aggressive retries, a race condition that crashed nodes, and slow control-plane calls caused thread starvation that spread to the regional API. Changes: larger spare-capacity buffers, retries that back off harder and prefer existing replicas, the race fixed, AZ-aware control-plane timeouts with load shedding, and a 10-day credit. Tags: [overload, partial failure, fan-out] [topics: ebs, replication, retries, capacity-planning, control-plane]. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/message/65648/

- **GitHub, 2012-09: MySQL cluster failover loop and split-brain** — A schema migration overloaded the new 3-node MySQL cluster (Pacemaker/Heartbeat/Percona Replication Manager). Health checks failed, and automatic failover moved the active role to a node with a cold InnoDB buffer pool. That node performed badly, so the role flipped back. The next day, taking Pacemaker out of maintenance mode segfaulted it and partitioned the cluster. Two masters were elected, one of them stale. Total downtime was 1h46m plus about 1h of heavy degradation. MySQL IDs fell out of sync with Redis, so events appeared on the wrong dashboards. 16 private repositories were visible to outsiders for 7 minutes. Changes: failover now happens only when a human starts it, investigation of buffer-pool warming on replicas, and an audit of the Pacemaker code path. Tags: [partial failure, multi-step consistency, ordering] [topics: mysql, failover, split-brain, cache-warming, schema-migration]. Source: https://github.blog/2012-09-14-github-availability-this-week/

- **Facebook, 2013-04: Scaling Memcache (NSDI paper)** — A peer-reviewed description of how Facebook runs memcached as a demand-filled look-aside cache that handles billions of requests per second. Leases solve two problems. They stop stale sets: a set is refused if the key was deleted after the lease was issued. They also tame thundering herds: by default a server issues at most one token per key every 10 seconds, and other clients briefly wait and retry. On herd-prone keys, leases cut the peak database query rate from 17K/s to 1.3K/s. Other designs: invalidations driven by the database commit log (the mcsqueal daemons), and a small "Gutter" pool (about 1% of servers) that takes over for failed hosts so misses don't hit the database. Tags: [hot spots, overload, ordering, fan-out] [topics: memcached, caching, cache-invalidation, thundering-herd, leases]. Source: https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi13/technical-sessions/presentation/nishtala

- **Microsoft Azure, 2014-11: Storage config change puts Blob front-ends into an infinite loop** — A performance change meant for Table storage front-ends was enabled across production at once. The required incremental "flighting" was skipped because tooling did not enforce it, and the switch was also turned on for Blob front-ends. A bug there sent some front-ends into an infinite loop. Storage connectivity was intermittent in several regions from 00:50 to 10:50 UTC on Nov 19. Recovery of the remaining VMs took until Nov 21. The change was reverted within 30 minutes, but the stuck front-ends had to be restarted. Changes: deployment tooling now enforces testing and staged rollout instead of relying on people to follow the policy. Tags: [partial failure, trust but verify] [topics: blob-storage, config-rollout, feature-flags, deployment-safety]. Source: https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/final-root-cause-analysis-and-improvement-areas-nov-18-azure-storage-service-interruption/

- **Jepsen (Elasticsearch), 2015-04: Elasticsearch 1.5.0 loses acknowledged writes** — Jepsen tests showed Elasticsearch 1.5.0 losing acknowledged documents under several faults. Intersecting partitions lost about 2.5%. Isolating a primary lost about 22% (209 of 947) in one run. Repeated process pauses lost about 9.3%. Under kill -9, about 10% were lost because writes were acknowledged before the translog fsync, which defaulted to every 5 seconds. Split-brain let two primaries accept writes for seconds, and writes were unavailable for a hardcoded ~90 seconds after an isolation. Recommendation: don't make a search index your system of record. Keep data in a safer database and continuously upsert it into the index. Tags: [partial failure, trust but verify, finding things fast] [topics: elasticsearch, search-indexing, split-brain, fsync, durability]. Source: https://aphyr.com/posts/323-call-me-maybe-elasticsearch-1-5-0

- **Sentry, 2015-07: Postgres transaction ID wraparound halts writes** — On Jul 20, 2015, Sentry's Postgres stopped accepting writes as it neared transaction-ID wraparound, a safety stop. Autovacuum settings were too lax, and one autovacuum never finished a large table. The service ran read-only from replicas for most of a US workday. Sentry flushed its event backlog and truncated a large event-to-rollup mapping table to free XIDs quickly. Changes: bigger hardware with separate WAL/data arrays, and aggressive autovacuum tuning (autovacuum_freeze_max_age 500M, 6 workers, 15s naptime, zero cost delay). They later learned that maintenance_work_mem is capped at 1GB for vacuum. Tags: [overload, trust but verify] [topics: postgres, vacuum, xid-wraparound, mvcc]. Source: https://blog.sentry.io/transaction-id-wraparound-in-postgres/

- **AWS, 2015-09: DynamoDB metadata service overload in US-East** — On Sunday Sep 20, a short network blip made many DynamoDB storage servers ask the metadata service for their partition membership at the same moment. Fast adoption of Global Secondary Indexes had made membership lists much bigger, so responses went past the servers' timeout. Those servers then took themselves out of service and retried, which overloaded the metadata service further. Customer error rates peaked around 55%. Most recovery came after about 4h50m (2:19–7:10am PDT), and SQS, Auto Scaling and CloudWatch were also affected. Changes: more metadata capacity, monitoring of membership size, less frequent membership requests with longer timeouts, and a plan to split the metadata service into many instances. Tags: [overload, fan-out, hot spots] [topics: dynamodb, metadata-service, retries, timeouts, capacity-planning]. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/message/5467D2/

- **Uber, 2016-07: Why Uber moved from Postgres to MySQL** — Uber described the Postgres limits it hit at scale. Each row update wrote new entries to every index (write amplification). Physical WAL replication carried all those writes across data centers. A 9.2 bug misapplied WAL on replicas during timeline switches, leaving superseded row versions visible as duplicates. Replication across major versions wasn't possible, so upgrades were painful (they stayed on 9.2). Corrupted replicas were rebuilt from a fresh master snapshot. Outcome: most databases moved to MySQL, usually behind the Schemaless sharding layer. Tags: [duplicates, multi-step consistency, trust but verify] [topics: postgres, mysql, replication, mvcc, write-amplification, upgrades]. Source: https://www.uber.com/en-US/blog/postgres-to-mysql-migration/

- **GitHub, 2016-08: gh-ost, triggerless online schema migrations for MySQL** — GitHub explained why trigger-based tools like pt-online-schema-change hurt them. Triggers add overhead to every write, compete for locks and metadata locks, and can't be paused without losing data. gh-ost instead tails the binary log and copies rows into a "ghost" table through a single serialized writer. It can throttle on replication lag or load, take runtime commands, and postpone cut-over. GitHub tests it by running no-op migrations on production replicas and comparing checksums of the original and ghost tables. Every table passed several of these before the tool ran on masters. Tags: [contention, multi-step consistency, trust but verify] [topics: mysql, schema-migration, binlog, throttling]. Source: https://github.blog/2016-08-01-gh-ost-github-s-online-migration-tool-for-mysql/

## 2017–2020

- **Stripe, 2017-02: Online migrations at scale** — Stripe moved hundreds of millions of Subscription objects out of Customer records into their own table without downtime. They used four steps: dual-write and backfill, switch reads, switch writes, then delete the old path. A sequential migration at 1 second per object would have taken over 3 years for 100M objects, so the backfill ran offline on Hadoop/Scalding. GitHub's Scientist library ran the old and new read paths side by side and flagged differences. Each code change stayed small, a few hundred lines at most, and dual writes were ramped up gradually. Tags: [multi-step consistency, trust but verify] [topics: data-migration, dual-write, backfill, verification]. Source: https://stripe.com/blog/online-migrations

- **GitLab, 2017-01: Accidental deletion of the production database** — A spam-driven load spike made replication lag until the primary had discarded WAL the secondary still needed. While rebuilding the secondary, an engineer wiped the data directory on the primary instead, about 300 GB. All the backup methods turned out to be broken. pg_dump had been failing silently because of a client/server version mismatch, and its failure emails were rejected by DMARC. Disk snapshots were not enabled. GitLab.com was down about 18 hours and restored from a 6-hour-old LVM snapshot. About 5,000 projects, 5,000 comments and 700 users were lost. Changes: hourly snapshots, work on WAL archiving and point-in-time recovery, automated restore testing, backup monitoring, and a named owner for data durability. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure] [topics: postgres, backups, replication, wal, human-error]. Source: https://about.gitlab.com/blog/2017/02/10/postmortem-of-database-outage-of-january-31/

- **AWS, 2017-02: S3 outage in US-East from a mistyped capacity command** — While debugging S3 billing, an engineer meant to remove a few servers but a mistyped argument removed many more. Among them were servers for the index subsystem (object metadata) and the placement subsystem. Both had to restart fully. That hadn't been done in years at this scale, and the integrity checks were slow. S3 in us-east-1 was down for about 4h17m (writes back last, at 1:54pm PST), affecting EC2 launches, EBS restores, Lambda and AWS's own status dashboard. Changes: the tool now removes capacity slowly and refuses to go below minimums, other tools were audited, and work on cells and index partitioning was sped up. Tags: [partial failure, trust but verify, finding things fast] [topics: s3, object-storage, metadata-index, operational-tooling, blast-radius]. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/message/41926/

- **GitHub, 2018-10: 43-second partition causes cross-coast MySQL failover and 24h degradation** — Network maintenance cut the US East hub off from the main East data center for 43 seconds. Orchestrator (Raft-based) promoted MySQL primaries on the West Coast before they had fully caught up. Writes then landed on both coasts and diverged, so a clean failback was impossible. GitHub chose to restore from backups and re-replicate instead. The service was degraded for 24h11m. More than 5M webhook events and 80K Pages builds queued, and about 200K webhook payloads expired. A few seconds of writes (954 on one busy cluster) had to be reconciled by hand. Changes: no cross-region primary promotion, per-component status reporting, and work toward active/active/active. Tags: [partial failure, multi-step consistency, ordering] [topics: mysql, orchestrator, failover, replication-lag, split-brain]. Source: https://github.blog/2018-10-30-oct21-post-incident-analysis/

- **Mailchimp (Mandrill), 2019-02: Postgres XID wraparound on a hot shard** — One Postgres shard took a disproportionate share of writes because of the hashing scheme. Its autovacuum fell behind, and the database hit the transaction-ID limit and shut down to protect itself. A standalone VACUUM was estimated to take days to weeks, so the team truncated the Search and Url tables to let it finish. The outage ran about 40.5 hours (Feb 4 05:35 to Feb 5 22:09 UTC), and only about 80% of queued email was sent during it. Changes: XID-wraparound alerting, refunds and credits, and incident-command training. Tags: [hot spots, overload, trust but verify] [topics: postgres, xid-wraparound, vacuum, sharding]. Source: https://mailchimp.com/what-we-learned-from-the-recent-mandrill-outage/

- **Stripe, 2019-07: Database shard can't elect a primary, then the rollback makes it worse** — Three months earlier, a database minor-version upgrade had introduced a latent failover bug. It appeared only when several nodes were stalled, and two nodes had quietly stopped reporting replication lag while still passing health checks. When the primary failed, one shard could not elect a new one and rejected writes. API workers blocked on it, and most API requests failed for about 27 minutes. Responders then rolled back the election protocol, which clashed with a recent config change and starved CPU on every shard. That caused a second outage of about 93 minutes. Changes: alerts on nodes that stop reporting lag, circuit-breaking, shard fault isolation, limits on client retries, and safer config changes during incidents. Tags: [partial failure, trust but verify, contention] [topics: database-failover, leader-election, health-checks, retries]. Source: https://stripe.com/rcas/2019-07-10

- **Monzo, 2019-07: Cassandra scale-up makes data look missing** — Monzo added 6 nodes to a 21-node Cassandra cluster with auto_bootstrap=false, expecting them to stay idle until data was streamed in. Instead they took ownership of token ranges immediately and answered reads with "not found". Balances and transactions looked wrong, and logins and payments failed. Most customers recovered in about 2 hours (13:10–15:08 BST), and full reconciliation by event replay took until 23:00. Testing with a single node had not exposed the quorum effect seen with six. Changes: setting corrected, runbooks for scale-ups, alerts on spikes in "row not found", and a plan to split the cluster. Tags: [partial failure, trust but verify] [topics: cassandra, cluster-membership, quorum, consistent-hashing]. Source: https://monzo.com/blog/2019/09/08/why-monzo-wasnt-working-on-july-29th

- **Jepsen (etcd), 2020-01: etcd 3.4.3 key-value is safe, locks are not** — Jepsen found etcd's key-value operations strict-serializable even under partitions, pauses, clock skew and membership changes. Its distributed locks did not give mutual exclusion, even in healthy clusters. With 2-second lease TTLs and periodic pauses, lock-protected updates lost about 18% of acknowledged writes. A bug also skipped re-checking the lease after a lock wait. Recommendation: use etcd locks for efficiency, not correctness, and have the protected resource enforce fencing tokens (for example the lock key's revision). Tags: [contention, ordering, trust but verify] [topics: etcd, distributed-locks, leases, fencing-tokens, raft]. Source: https://jepsen.io/analyses/etcd-3.4.3

- **Discord, 2020-02: GC spikes in the Read States cache, rewritten in Rust** — Read States, a hot-path service that tracks which messages each user has read, kept a large in-memory LRU cache in Go. Latency and CPU spiked about every 2 minutes because Go forces a garbage collection at least that often, scanning the huge cache. A Rust rewrite had no GC spikes and beat Go on latency, CPU and memory. That let them raise the cache to 8 million Read States per server. Tags: [hot spots, overload] [topics: caching, lru, garbage-collection, latency]. Source: https://discord.com/blog/why-discord-is-switching-from-go-to-rust

- **Jepsen (MongoDB), 2020-05: MongoDB 4.2.6 transactions violate snapshot isolation** — Even with read concern snapshot and write concern majority, Jepsen saw read skew, cyclic information flow, duplicate writes, and a transaction seeing its own future writes. Transactions ignored database- and collection-level concerns and fell back to local / w:1, which allowed acknowledged writes to be lost during partitions. Retries ignored the retryWrites setting, which is a likely source of the duplicates. MongoDB traced the anomalies to a transaction-retry bug (SERVER-48307) fixed in 4.2.8. Recommendation: set concerns explicitly on every transaction and don't assume "full ACID" means serializable. Tags: [duplicates, multi-step consistency, trust but verify] [topics: mongodb, transactions, isolation-levels, retries]. Source: https://jepsen.io/analyses/mongodb-4.2.6

- **Jepsen (PostgreSQL), 2020-06: PostgreSQL 12.3 serializable isn't serializable** — Jepsen found that SERIALIZABLE transactions could show G2-item anti-dependency cycles under normal operation with concurrent inserts and updates. The cause was a conflict-detection bug that, with three concurrent transactions, credited an update to the wrong transaction ID. It had been present since 9.5 and was patched for the August 2020 minor releases. The report also notes that Postgres's REPEATABLE READ is really snapshot isolation and allows write skew. Tags: [contention, multi-step consistency, trust but verify] [topics: postgres, isolation-levels, serializable, ssi]. Source: https://jepsen.io/analyses/postgresql-12.3

- **Cloudflare, 2020-11: Partially failed switch splits etcd, triggering a cascade of database failovers** — A switch failed in a way that kept control protocols (LACP, BGP) up while dropping data-plane traffic. etcd nodes ended up with conflicting views of the cluster and couldn't elect a leader, so the cluster became read-only. Cluster management treated this as failure and promoted replicas in two Postgres clusters. A bug forced every replica to rebuild, which left the auth database without its read replica and overloaded it. API success fell as low as 75%, and the dashboard was up to 80x slower, for 6h33m. Changes: the rebuild-on-promotion bug fixed, slower auto-promotion, and continued work on sessions that can fail over across data centers. Tags: [partial failure, overload] [topics: etcd, postgres, failover, network-partition, auto-remediation]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/a-byzantine-failure-in-the-real-world/

## 2021–2023

- **Shopify, 2021-02: Monotonic reads across MySQL replicas** — Replicas with different lag let related queries in one request see data that didn't fit together, for example a row present in one table but missing from a related one. Shopify rejected two options: tight consistency (wait for every replica, too costly) and GTID-based causal consistency (too complex to operate). They built monotonic-read routing in their ProxySQL fork instead. A caller passes a consistent_read_id. It is hashed (modulo the server list, with weights) to pin a series of reads to one replica, and ProxySQL rebalancing is disabled for those requests to avoid a routing bug. Tags: [ordering, multi-step consistency] [topics: mysql, read-replicas, replication-lag, proxysql, consistency-models]. Source: https://shopify.engineering/read-consistency-database-replicas

- **Notion, 2021-10: Sharding Postgres at Notion** — Postgres VACUUM kept stalling on the monolith, and transaction-ID wraparound was becoming a real risk. Notion sharded at the application level by workspace ID into 480 logical shards (Postgres schemas) on 32 physical databases. 480 was chosen because it has many divisors, so shards can be redistributed evenly. The migration had four stages. Writes went to both systems through an audit log and catch-up script, after logical replication couldn't handle the block table. A 3-day backfill on a 96-CPU machine compared record versions. Sampled comparisons and dark reads checked for differences. The final switch took about 5 minutes of downtime. Tags: [hot spots, multi-step consistency, trust but verify] [topics: postgres, sharding, data-migration, dual-write, vacuum]. Source: https://www.notion.com/blog/sharding-postgres-at-notion

- **Roblox, 2021-10: 73-hour outage from Consul streaming contention and a bloated BoltDB freelist** — Roblox had enabled Consul 1.10's new streaming feature on a busy service. Under heavy read/write load it caused contention on a single Go channel, made worse by 128-core NUMA hosts. KV write latency rose to about 2 seconds. Separately, Consul's BoltDB Raft log store had a freelist of nearly a million page IDs (about 7.8MB) that was rewritten on every append, which slowed leaders. Every backend service depended on that one Consul cluster, and so did monitoring. The outage lasted 73 hours (Oct 28–31) for about 50M daily users, with no data loss. Changes: telemetry no longer depends on Consul, dedicated Consul clusters, cleanup of obsolete KV data, a move to bbolt, and multi-AZ work. Tags: [contention, overload, finding things fast] [topics: consul, boltdb, raft, service-discovery, kv-store]. Source: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2022/01/roblox-return-to-service-10-28-10-31-2021/

- **Slack, 2022-02: Cache loss turns a sharded lookup into a database-wide scatter query** — A gradual Consul agent upgrade removed memcached nodes from the catalog. Slack's cache control plane (Mcrib) replaced them with empty nodes, and the hit rate dropped. Group DM membership was cached by channel ID, but Vitess was sharded by user. Each cache miss therefore became a scatter query across every shard. The database overloaded and couldn't refill the cache, and client retries added more load. Many users couldn't boot Slack starting just after 6am PT. Changes: a safer Consul rollout, the query moved to a channel-sharded table, an audit of other cache-fronted scatter queries, and Mcrib hardening. Tags: [fan-out, overload, hot spots] [topics: memcached, vitess, mcrouter, consul, retries, cache-stampede]. Source: https://slack.engineering/slacks-incident-on-2-22-22/

- **GitHub, 2022-03: Resource contention on the shared mysql1 cluster** — Four outages in one week (Mar 16, 17, 22, 23), each lasting about 2.5 to 5.5 hours. Peak-hour load on the shared mysql1 primary, together with a poorly performing query, maxed out the database proxy's connections. Writes failed across git operations, webhooks, PRs, Actions and more. Each recovery was a primary failover. One was caused by enabling memory profiling on the proxy. Changes: an index for the bad query, webhook throttling at peak, moving traffic off mysql1, and continued partitioning and sharding of the main database. Tags: [contention, overload, hot spots] [topics: mysql, connection-limits, database-proxy, failover, sharding]. Source: https://github.blog/2022-03-23-an-update-on-recent-service-disruptions/

- **Atlassian, 2022-04: Deletion script removes 883 customer sites** — A cleanup script was meant to delete a deprecated app by app ID. It was given site IDs instead, and the deletion API accepted either kind of ID without asking which was meant. Each ID permanently deleted a whole site. Peer review checked the API call but not the IDs, and staging didn't contain those IDs. 883 sites (775 customers) running Jira, Confluence and Opsgenie went down for up to 14 days (Apr 5–18). Restores worked within the 1-hour RPO (at most 5 minutes of data lost) but far missed the RTO. Multi-site restores weren't automated, and the first approach took about 70 steps and 48h per batch. Changes: bulk deletions blocked, universal soft delete, automated multi-site disaster recovery with regular drills, and off-instance copies of customer contacts. Tags: [trust but verify, multi-step consistency] [topics: backups, disaster-recovery, soft-delete, multi-tenant, rpo-rto]. Source: https://www.atlassian.com/engineering/post-incident-review-april-2022-outage

- **Discord, 2023-03: How Discord stores trillions of messages (Cassandra to ScyllaDB)** — Discord's messages cluster had grown from 12 Cassandra nodes in 2017 to 177 by 2022. It suffered hot partitions (a busy channel slowed quorum queries cluster-wide), GC pauses, and compaction backlogs. Rust "data services" were added in front of it. They coalesce concurrent reads of the same row into one query and route by channel ID with consistent hashing. A Rust migrator copied data at up to 3.2M messages/s, and the final 0.0001% was blocked by tombstones until those ranges were compacted. Reads were mirrored to both databases to compare results. After the move to 72 ScyllaDB nodes, p99 historical-fetch latency fell from 40–125ms to 15ms. Tags: [hot spots, fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: cassandra, scylladb, data-migration, request-coalescing, compaction]. Source: https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-stores-trillions-of-messages

- **Tarsnap, 2023-07: Rebuilding a log-structured backup service from S3** — The EC2 instance hosting Tarsnap's central server failed, and its filesystem was corrupted. The author built a replacement and replayed every metadata header from the S3-backed log-structured store. Replay hit a 2014-era bug involving machines re-registered to a new account, and a resume attempt later skipped the S3 download step. Downtime was about 26 hours (Jul 2–3), and replay was limited by disk throughput. Read-only comparisons against the old state were run before reopening. Only the last few seconds of data were lost, and all accounts got a 50% storage credit. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure] [topics: backups, s3, log-structured-storage, recovery, ebs]. Source: https://mail.tarsnap.com/tarsnap-announce/msg00050.html

- **Cloudflare, 2023-11: Data center power loss exposes single-site Kafka/ClickHouse dependencies** — Power failed at the PDX-04 data center (generators tripped on a ground fault, UPS ran out after about 4 minutes). Cloudflare's "HA" control plane turned out to depend on Kafka and ClickHouse that ran only in that facility. Newer products' databases had never been required to meet HA standards. Most control-plane services moved to the EU disaster-recovery site in about 6 hours, but analytics and logs took about 41 hours. Some logs were lost permanently, and retried API calls created a thundering herd that needed rate limiting. Changes ("Code Orange"): GA products must use the HA cluster and have a tested DR plan, and chaos tests now remove each core data center entirely. Tags: [partial failure, trust but verify, overload] [topics: disaster-recovery, kafka, clickhouse, high-availability, dependencies]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/post-mortem-on-cloudflare-control-plane-and-analytics-outage/

- **Jepsen (MySQL), 2023-12: MySQL 8.0.34 repeatable read allows lost updates** — On a single healthy node, InnoDB's REPEATABLE READ produced write-skew-like cycles, read skew and lost updates (198 lost updates in one run of about 9K transactions). It even showed reads that changed within a single transaction. On AWS RDS Multi-AZ clusters, even SERIALIZABLE showed anomalies, likely because replica_preserve_commit_order defaults to off there. Recommendation: use SERIALIZABLE or SELECT ... FOR UPDATE for read-modify-write code (common in ORMs), and turn on commit-order preservation on RDS. Tags: [contention, ordering, trust but verify] [topics: mysql, isolation-levels, lost-update, rds, replication]. Source: https://jepsen.io/analyses/mysql-8.0.34

## 2024–2025

- **Figma, 2024-03: Horizontally sharding Postgres** — Figma's database stack grew almost 100x from 2020. After vertical partitioning, some tables reached several terabytes and billions of rows, causing vacuum problems and pushing against RDS IOPS limits. The team built in-house horizontal sharding on RDS Postgres. "Colos" are groups of tables sharing a shard key, so joins and transactions within one key still work. Postgres views provided logical sharding before any data moved, which made rollback safe. A Go DBProxy parses and routes queries and runs limited scatter-gather. The first table took about 9 months and went live in September 2023 with about 10 seconds of partial unavailability. Tags: [hot spots, fan-out, multi-step consistency] [topics: postgres, sharding, query-routing, rds, vacuum]. Source: https://www.figma.com/blog/how-figmas-databases-team-lived-to-tell-the-scale/

- **Google Cloud, 2024-05: Customer's VMware private cloud auto-deleted after a blank parameter** — In early 2023, Google operators deployed UniSuper's GCVE private cloud with an internal tool and left one parameter blank. The system quietly applied a default 1-year term and later deleted the private cloud automatically, with no notification, because the deletion wasn't customer-initiated. Recovery took several days of round-the-clock work and relied on the customer's backups in Cloud Storage plus third-party backup software. Other customers and services were unaffected. Changes: the internal tool was deprecated, all private clouds were reviewed, and the deletion-marking behavior was corrected. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: backups, disaster-recovery, defaults, deletion-safety]. Source: https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure/details-of-google-cloud-gcve-incident

- **Cloudflare, 2024-11: Blank config makes the log pipeline fan out 40x and lose 55% of logs** — A bug in config generation produced an empty Logpush configuration and was reverted within 5 minutes. Logfwdr treated "no config" as a reason to fail open and forward logs for every customer, a failsafe designed when traffic was much lower. Buftee, which keeps one buffer per customer, jumped from about 1M to 40M buffers. Its overload protections had never been configured, so it stopped responding and needed a full restart. About 55% of customer logs were lost over about 3.5 hours. Changes: alerts for this kind of misconfiguration, a fix with tests for Logfwdr, and new "overload tests". Tags: [fan-out, overload, trust but verify] [topics: logging-pipeline, fail-open, config-validation, buffering]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-incident-on-november-14-2024-resulting-in-lost-logs/

- **Cloudflare, 2025-02: Abuse remediation disables the R2 object storage gateway** — While handling a phishing report, an operator disabled the whole production R2 Gateway service instead of a single bucket. Instagram denormalized the count into a column updated in the same transaction as the like row, and used memcache lease-get so only one web server refilled a deleted key ("first miss") while others got a stale value or waited ("hot miss"). Tags: [hot spots, overload, duplicates] [topics: counters, denormalization, memcache-lease, thundering-herd, multi-region]. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20250121100731/https://instagram-engineering.com/instagration-pt-2-scaling-our-infrastructure-to-multiple-data-centers-5745cbad7834
- **Harmonix, 2015-12 to 2016-02: Rock Band 4 wipes all leaderboard scores after scoring exploits** — Exploits included drummers double-deploying Overdrive at the end of fills, calibration/speed abuse and the "Band Overdrive Squeeze". Because the fixes affected almost every song, instrument and difficulty, all leaderboard high scores were wiped with the February 2016 update (servers down from about 6am ET); local Full Combo data was kept, and code was changed to allow targeted per-song wipes in future. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: leaderboard, cheating, score-recompute, rollback]. Sources: https://www.harmonixmusic.com/blog/january-update-blog and https://www.harmonixmusic.com/blog/rock-band-4-february-update-patch-notes
- **Ubisoft, 2019-02: Rainbow Six Siege MMR rollback for matches with banned cheaters** — From Y4S1, banning a cheater reverses MMR gains and losses for all players in all of the cheater's matches that season (wins and losses alike), never awarding MMR above a player's season maximum. A 7-day window was tested and rejected as inconsistent; the feature was tested silently for a season first. Tags: [trust but verify, multi-step consistency] [topics: leaderboard, ranking, cheating, event-replay]. Source: https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/rainbow-six/siege/news-updates/Fhj616gBRK6Ny33KzTtgL
- **Twitter, 2021-10: ad engagement aggregation moves from Lambda to Kappa (engineering write-up)** — About 400 billion events a day; the interaction and engagement aggregates are the source of truth for ads revenue. The old Summingbird/TSAR design (Heron streaming plus hourly batch into Manhattan, real time in Nighthawk) lost events when back-pressured Heron containers were restarted. New design: on-prem relay to Pub/Sub at least once with a UUID per event, Dataflow dedup within a timed window plus aggregation, Bigtable serving, latency up to about 10 s. Validation: >95% exact match against batch counts; most of the <5% gap was late events the batch pipelines discarded. Tags: [duplicates, ordering, multi-step consistency] [topics: kappa, lambda, dataflow, pubsub, dedup, ads]. Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20231209122441/https://blog.twitter.com/engineering/en_us/topics/infrastructure/2021/processing-billions-of-events-in-real-time-at-twitter-
- **Uber, 2021-09: exactly-once ad event processing with Flink, Kafka and Pinot (engineering write-up)** — Ads on Uber Eats: Flink filters, dedups with keyed state, and aggregates clicks and impressions in one-minute tumbling windows; output goes to Kafka uncommitted and is committed by two-phase commit on each checkpoint (2-minute interval, called the main latency bottleneck); consumers use read_committed. Each record carries a UUID used as an idempotency key by the budget service, for Hive dedup and for Pinot upserts. Two regions run active-active; a union job merges both regions' output because Pinot does not replicate across regions; Kafka retention is 3 days. Tags: [duplicates, multi-step consistency] [topics: exactly-once, flink, kafka-transactions, pinot-upsert, ads]. Source: https://www.uber.com/en-US/blog/real-time-exactly-once-ad-event-processing/

## Recommendation and media pipelines (appended during the sdi-044 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

- **ByteDance, 2022: Monolith online training beats batch in a live A/B test (paper)** — On a production ads model, online training improved online serving AUC over batch training by 14.0% to 18.1% on each of seven days. ## Hot inventory rows and availability indexes (appended during the sdi-049 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

- **Alibaba, 2012-11: Double Eleven inventory row overloads and oversells** — Inventory deduction for a popular item makes every buyer update one row; under row-lock contention throughput collapsed, the inventory database ran out of capacity at midnight on Double Eleven 2012, and Alibaba reports a large number of oversold orders. For 2013 the database kernel team added two switchable fixes to AliSQL, queuing updates to the same row and a concurrency-control change; a 2016 rework made inventory deduction about 10 times faster again. Tags: [contention, hot spots, overload] [topics: flash sale, inventory, row locks, mysql]. Source: https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/a-decade-of-evolution-of-alibabas-databases---part-1_594444
- **Alibaba, 2018-11: Singles' Day transactions jump 122x in one second (SIGMOD paper)** — The X-Engine paper reports one online database cluster seeing transactions rise about 122 times in the first second after midnight on 11 November 2018, with Alibaba's clusters processing up to 491,000 sales transactions per second, and describes large numbers of hot records that can overwhelm buffers. Tags: [overload, hot spots] [topics: flash sale, lsm storage engine, peak load]. Source: https://users.cs.utah.edu/~lifeifei/papers/sigmod-xengine.pdf
- **Airbnb, 2025: price and availability churn rules out an HNSW index (engineering write-up)** — Adding embedding-based retrieval to search, Airbnb found the volume of real-time price and availability updates made an HNSW index's memory footprint grow too large, and HNSW combined with the geographic filters most searches use gave poor latency. It chose IVF, whose cluster assignments act as an ordinary search filter, trading slightly lower recall. Tags: [finding things fast, hot spots] [topics: search, vector index, ivf, hnsw, availability]. Source: https://airbnb.tech/ai-ml/embedding-based-retrieval-for-airbnb-search/

## Time series database write paths (appended during the sdi-053 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

- **Grafana Labs, 2019-07: pod priority preemption cascades through Cortex ingesters** — On July 19, 2019, a day after new Kubernetes pod priority classes were deployed, a new dedicated Cortex cluster went out without them, so its ingesters got the default "medium" priority, higher than production ingesters, which had none. New ingesters preempted a production ingester, its replacement preempted another, and eventually all production ingesters were preempted. Ingesters hold up to 12 hours of data and replicate each series to three of them, so writes failed. Impact lasted about 26 minutes with no data loss, because customers' Prometheus servers buffered remote write in their WAL and replayed it. A more than 10x QPS spike from client retries also OOM-killed the authenticating reverse proxies. Changes: "medium" no longer the default, production ingesters given high priority, more reviewers and a design document for cluster-wide config changes. Tags: [partial failure, overload] [topics: cortex, prometheus, tsdb, kubernetes, ingesters, retries]. Source: https://grafana.com/blog/2019/07/24/how-a-production-outage-was-caused-using-kubernetes-pod-priorities/

## Document store sharding (appended during the sdi-054 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

- **Foursquare, 2010-10: one MongoDB shard outgrows its RAM** — About two months after moving from one 66 GB-RAM EC2 instance to two shards keyed by user ID (about 33 GB each), uneven check-in activity grew shard0 to about 67 GB while the other held about 50 GB. Once shard0's data exceeded RAM, queries hit disk, a backlog built and the site went down on two consecutive days. Adding a third shard and moving about 5% of the data did not help because 300-byte check-in documents left the old pages fragmented; a roughly four-hour offline compaction (repairDatabase) on shard0 brought it back. Changes: more shards, compacted secondaries promoted (about 20 GB RAM per shard), and 10gen planned online compaction and size-aware chunk balancing. Tags: [hot spots, overload] [topics: mongodb, sharding, shard-key, working-set, fragmentation]. Source: https://groups.google.com/g/mongodb-user/c/UoqU8ofp134
- **Apache Spark, 2018-01: SPARK-23207, retried repartition returns wrong rows** — `repartition` uses round-robin partitioning, whose output depends on input row order. In a job with several shuffles, an executor loss made retried tasks see rows in a different order, so retried downstream tasks produced different data; the reporter's job returned 931,532 rows instead of 1,000,000. Blocker, labelled correctness, affecting 1.6 to 2.3; fixed in 2.1.4, 2.2.3 and 2.3.0. Spark 4.1 later added checksum-based full retry of shuffle stages to avoid incorrect results. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure] [topics: spark, lineage, shuffle, determinism, retries]. Source: https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/SPARK-23207

## Added 2026-10-18 (Low-Level Design source upgrade)

Found by the lesson writers during the LLD upgrade. Each URL was opened on 2026-10-18.

- **Slack, API pagination: from offsets to opaque cursors** — Endpoints that returned hundreds of records grew to hundreds of thousands. LIMIT/OFFSET made the database read offset+count rows, and pages repeated items as new data arrived. Slack introduced cursor, limit and next_cursor (empty at the end), with base64-encoded opaque cursors that can carry several shard positions, and gave up total counts and page jumps. Tags: [finding things fast, ordering] [topics: pagination, cursors, api-design]. Source: https://slack.engineering/evolving-api-pagination-at-slack/

- **Apache Commons DBCP, 2018-08: JDBC 4.1 calls break old drivers with AbstractMethodError** — DBCP 2.5.0 began calling Java 7 / JDBC 4.1 methods such as Connection.getSchema(). Drivers built for JDBC 4.0 still loaded, but the JVM threw AbstractMethodError when the missing method was called. 2.6.0 detected the condition and used null for schema values. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: jdbc, interface-evolution, binary-compatibility]. Source: https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/DBCP-518

- **GitLab, 2021-06 to 2021-09: PostgreSQL subtransactions stall GitLab.com** — Intermittent minutes-long database stalls caused HTTP 500s and a CI backlog, with queries waiting on SubtransControlLock (SubtransSLRU in PG13+). A long-running transaction plus heavy writes triggered it, even though the app used at most about 10 nested savepoints; a transaction with more than 64 subtransactions overflows the per-backend cache. GitLab removed all SAVEPOINT use and added an alert on any savepoint. Tags: [contention, overload] [topics: postgres, savepoints, subtransactions]. Source: https://about.gitlab.com/blog/2021/09/29/why-we-spent-the-last-month-eliminating-postgresql-subtransactions/
