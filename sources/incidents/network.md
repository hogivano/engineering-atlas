# Network incidents catalogue

Real, publicly documented incidents and engineering write-ups covering DNS, BGP, CDNs, load balancers, TLS/certificates, proxies, rate limiting, traffic spikes and time/clock bugs. Every entry links to a primary source (the company's own postmortem, status page or engineering blog). Each URL was opened and its details checked in October 2026. Summaries are in our own words.

Problem shapes used in tags: contention, duplicates, ordering, fan-out, hot spots, overload, partial failure, multi-step consistency, finding things fast, trust but verify.

## Time and clocks

- **Microsoft Azure, 2012-02: Leap-day certificate bug takes down compute management** — On 29 February 2012 the guest agent tried to issue a transfer certificate valid until "same day next year", which came out as 29 Feb 2013, a date that doesn't exist. Certificate creation failed, hosts read the timeouts as hardware faults, and the false failures cascaded across clusters. Service management was switched off worldwide for about 10.5 hours, and the last services were confirmed healthy about 34 hours after the trigger. Storage and SQL Azure stayed up. A fix rollout then caused a second incident: an old host agent paired with an incompatible network plugin cut VMs off in seven clusters. Microsoft fixed the date logic, promised better time-related testing and finer-grained service management controls, and gave a 33% service credit. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure, fan-out] [topics: time, leap day, certificates, health checks, cascading failure]. Source: https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/summary-of-windows-azure-service-disruption-on-feb-29th-2012/
- **Cloudflare, 2017-01: Leap second makes RRDNS panic** — At midnight UTC on 1 January 2017 the leap second made the wall clock step backwards, so Go's (then non-monotonic) `time.Now()` produced negative upstream round-trip times. The smoothed averages went negative and reached `rand.Int63n()`, which panics on negative input. CNAME resolutions failed on some machines. At peak about 0.2% of DNS queries and under 1% of HTTP requests were affected, for roughly 6h45m. Cloudflare patched RRDNS to discard negative intervals, rolled the fix out canary-first, and audited other code that measures time intervals. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure] [topics: time, leap second, monotonic clock, dns, go]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/how-and-why-the-leap-second-affected-cloudflare-dns/

## DNS

- **GitHub, 2014-01: DDoS hardening breaks DNS, then provisioning wipes records** — A Puppet change that limited which hosts could send DNS traffic restarted the authoritative server but not the caching one, and the caching server kept querying an address that no longer answered. After a reload, a circular dependency between the provisioning service and DNS produced zone files built from unchecked API output, and many records disappeared (NXDOMAIN). Overloaded fileservers made recovery slower. The impact was 42 minutes of full downtime, plus about 1h35m more for some repositories. GitHub decoupled internal and external DNS, broke the provisioning–DNS cycle, added output checks before records are removed, and used cgroups to cap process resource use. Tags: [multi-step consistency, trust but verify, overload] [topics: dns, configuration management, circular dependency, zone files]. Source: https://github.blog/2014-01-18-dns-outage-post-mortem
- **Slack, 2021-09: DNSSEC rollout leaves slack.com unresolvable for some users** — Slack turned on DNSSEC for slack.com, which has a wildcard record. Route 53 signed NSEC responses for wildcard names without the record-type bitmap, so validating resolvers that asked for AAAA cached the answer as "this name has no records at all", and later A lookups failed too. Rolling back made things worse: resolvers had cached the DS record for up to 24 hours, so they returned SERVFAIL. The outage lasted about 24 hours for under 1% of users, with uneven regional impact. AWS fixed the NSEC bug, resolver operators flushed their caches, and Slack now tests domains with wildcards before such rollouts. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure, multi-step consistency] [topics: dns, dnssec, caching, ttl, route53]. Source: https://slack.engineering/what-happened-during-slacks-dnssec-rollout/
- **Cloudflare, 2023-10: Root-zone ZONEMD record leaves 1.1.1.1 serving an expired zone** — On 21 September the root zone gained a new record type, ZONEMD, which 1.1.1.1's static_zone parser rejected. The resolvers quietly kept the last root zone they had loaded. When that copy's DNSSEC signatures expired on 4 October, validation failed and SERVFAILs climbed to 15% of queries, against a usual 3%. The incident lasted about 4 hours and hit large data centers hardest. The first override didn't reach forwarded queries. Cloudflare added staleness alerts, capped how long stale root data can be used (per RFC 8806), and reworked root-zone ingestion. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure] [topics: dns, dnssec, root zone, stale data, parsing]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/1-1-1-1-lookup-failures-on-october-4th-2023/
- **Square, 2023-09: Firewall change plus DNS upgrade overloads internal DNS** — A host-firewall rule meant to enable region-to-region traffic expanded into a huge ruleset on every node and made nodes unstable. At the same time a DNS service upgrade sent unexpected load to the internal DNS servers, and they failed. Once node-local DNS caches expired, services could no longer find their dependencies, and the debugging and recovery tools were down too. Payments were unavailable for about 15.5 hours. Square is moving DNS onto isolated infrastructure, reducing deploy and platform dependencies, and expanding offline payments. Tags: [overload, finding things fast, partial failure] [topics: dns, service discovery, firewall, caching, blast radius]. Source: https://developer.squareup.com/blog/incident-summary-2023-09-07/
- **GitHub, 2024-10: Database migration breaks one site's DNS** — DNS at one GitHub site stopped resolving lookups after a database migration, and attempts to recover the database cascaded into the site's DNS systems. Repointing the site to another site's DNS helped inside the site but broke traffic from the healthy sites. Temporary DNS resolution finally restored it. 25% of Actions users saw delays over 5 minutes, 4% of Copilot users were degraded, and code search failed entirely for about 4 hours, across roughly 19 hours in all. GitHub said it is strengthening this infrastructure's resiliency and automation. Tags: [partial failure, finding things fast] [topics: dns, database migration, cascading failure]. Source: https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-availability-report-october-2024
- **AWS, 2025-10: DynamoDB DNS automation race empties the regional endpoint** — Two independent DNS Enactors raced. A delayed one applied an old plan after a newer one had landed, and cleanup then deleted that old plan, leaving `dynamodb.us-east-1.amazonaws.com` with no IP addresses. Recovery needed manual intervention, and the effects cascaded: EC2 instance launches failed (DropletWorkflow Manager recovery) and NLB connections errored. Impact lasted about 14.5 hours across Lambda, STS, ECS/EKS, Redshift and more. AWS disabled the Planner/Enactor automation worldwide, plans a velocity limit on NLB capacity removal, and plans queue-based throttling for EC2 recovery. Tags: [ordering, multi-step consistency, fan-out] [topics: dns, race condition, automation, us-east-1, dynamodb]. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/message/101925/
- **Cloudflare, 2025-07: Latent topology config withdraws 1.1.1.1 globally** — On 6 June a configuration change for a not-yet-live service accidentally included the 1.1.1.1 resolver prefixes. It had no visible effect at the time. On 14 July, adding an offline test location triggered a global refresh, which mapped the resolver prefixes to that single offline site, and every location withdrew them. 1.1.1.1 was down worldwide for 62 minutes, and about 23% of edge servers needed manual rebinding. An unrelated BGP hijack of 1.1.1.0/24 during the incident was not the cause. Cloudflare is retiring the legacy addressing system and moving to progressive, health-checked deployments for addressing changes. Tags: [trust but verify, partial failure] [topics: dns, anycast, bgp, configuration, staged rollout]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-1-1-1-1-incident-on-july-14-2025/

## BGP and routing

- **Cloudflare, 2013-03: Flowspec rule crashes every edge router** — During a DDoS against a customer's DNS, Cloudflare's attack profiler reported packet sizes of about 99,9xx bytes, which is impossible on their network. An operator pushed a Juniper Flowspec rule to drop those sizes, and Flowspec carried it to every edge router. No packet ever matched, but the routers ran out of RAM and crashed, and some needed physical reboots. All Cloudflare services were down for about an hour. Cloudflare planned more testing of Flowspec filters and scoping rules to affected sites only. Tags: [fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: bgp, flowspec, ddos mitigation, routers, global push]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/todays-outage-post-mortem-82515/
- **Google Cloud, 2016-04: Bad config push withdraws every GCE IP block** — An engineer removed an unused IP block, but the change reached only one of two config files. A latent bug then had the management software drop all GCE IP blocks rather than fall back to the last good config. The canary correctly flagged the result as unsafe, but a second bug kept that verdict from stopping the progressive push. Inbound traffic to GCE fell by more than 95% across all regions for 18 minutes. Google added semantic checks for required IP blocks, before/after announcement comparison, and network-path monitoring, and issued service credits. Tags: [trust but verify, multi-step consistency] [topics: bgp, canary, configuration push, anycast]. Source: https://status.cloud.google.com/incident/compute/16007
- **Cloudflare (analysing AWS Route 53), 2018-04: BGP hijack of DNS used to steal cryptocurrency** — eNet (AS10297) announced more-specific prefixes of Amazon's Route 53 address space for about two hours. The hijacking servers answered queries only for myetherwallet.com, pointing it at a phishing site. Resolvers that reached them, including some 1.1.1.1 PoPs, cached the poisoned answers, so users who never saw the bad route were affected too. Victims who clicked through a self-signed certificate warning lost credentials and funds. Cloudflare's recommendations: DNSSEC, HSTS, RPKI/ROA and IRR-based filtering. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: bgp hijack, dns, rpki, dnssec, tls]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/bgp-leaks-and-crypto-currencies/
- **Cloudflare (on Verizon/DQE), 2019-06: BGP optimizer route leak** — DQE's Noction BGP optimizer split received prefixes into more-specific routes and leaked them to its customer Allegheny Technologies, which passed them to Verizon (AS701). Verizon propagated them unfiltered. Because more-specific routes always win, large parts of the Internet sent traffic for Cloudflare, AWS, Linode and others through a small network. Cloudflare lost about 15% of its global traffic at the worst point. Recommended fixes: max-prefix limits, IRR-based filtering, RPKI origin validation and MANRS. Tags: [trust but verify, hot spots] [topics: bgp, route leak, rpki, more-specific prefixes]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/how-verizon-and-a-bgp-optimizer-knocked-large-parts-of-the-internet-offline-today/
- **Cloudflare, 2020-07: One-line backbone config turns Atlanta into a traffic sink** — While relieving backbone congestion, an engineer deactivated the wrong line. Instead of the policy term, the prefix-list condition was removed, so an Atlanta router advertised all its BGP routes with local-preference 200, beating the 100 used for local routes. Traffic from about 20 locations was pulled to Atlanta and overloaded it, and network traffic dropped about 50% for 27 minutes. Cloudflare applied a global backbone change, planned max-prefix limits on backbone sessions, and changed local-preference so local routes can't be outbid this way. Tags: [hot spots, overload] [topics: bgp, local preference, backbone, configuration]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-outage-on-july-17-2020/
- **Discord, 2020-11: Fiber cut plus BGP leak breaks connectivity in Europe** — Physical damage to a backbone fiber run upstream of Discord and its CDN coincided with a BGP table leak. The leak made two Tier 1 peers send traffic over the damaged path and over links too small for it. Users in France, Italy and Turkey, mainly on the Free and TurkNet ISPs, had connection problems for nearly two hours. Engineers routed around the damaged fiber, and routing was corrected once the leak was found. Tags: [partial failure, hot spots] [topics: bgp, route leak, fiber, peering]. Source: https://discordstatus.com/incidents/21b3s0pvbszf
- **Meta (Facebook), 2021-10: Backbone command withdraws its own DNS from the Internet** — A command meant to assess global backbone capacity disconnected every backbone link, and a bug in the audit tool let it run. The DNS servers then saw they could not reach the data centers and withdrew their BGP anycast advertisements as designed, so facebook.com could not be resolved anywhere. Internal tools failed too, so engineers had to recover on site, and services were brought back in stages to avoid power and load surges. Meta planned to add global backbone-loss scenarios to its "storm" drills. Tags: [trust but verify, fan-out, partial failure] [topics: bgp, dns, anycast, backbone, out-of-band access]. Source: https://engineering.fb.com/2021/10/05/networking-traffic/outage-details/
- **Cloudflare, 2022-06: Reordered BGP policy terms withdraw prefixes at 19 busy data centers** — A change to spine-router policies placed a catch-all reject term ahead of the terms that advertise site-local prefixes. Those prefixes were withdrawn at 19 Multi-Colo PoP sites, which are about 4% of locations but carry about 50% of HTTP requests. Engineers lost access, and the internal load balancer could no longer spread traffic between servers. The outage lasted about 1h15m. Cloudflare added MCP-specific staged rollout, redesigned the policy to prevent ordering mistakes, and added automatic commit-confirm rollback. Tags: [ordering, hot spots] [topics: bgp, router policy, staged rollout, anycast]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-outage-on-june-21-2022/
- **Cloudflare, 2024-06: 1.1.1.1 hijack and route leak** — Eletronet (AS267613) originated 1.1.1.1/32, and one Tier 1 provider accepted it as a blackhole route, even though Cloudflare's ROA allows nothing longer than /24. Separately, Nova (AS262504) leaked 1.1.1.0/24 upstream, and an exchange redistributed it widely. Users on more than 300 networks in 70 countries could not reach 1.1.1.1 or saw high latency, for about 3.5 hours (hijack) and 7.5 hours (leak), though the overall share of users affected was small. Cloudflare disabled the peering, expanded its leak detection, and pushes for RPKI validation, ASPA and RPKI-aware blackhole filtering. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: bgp hijack, route leak, rpki, anycast dns]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-1111-incident-on-june-27-2024/

## Datacenter and cloud network fabric

- **GitHub, 2012-12: Switch upgrade triggers split-brain in fileserver pairs** — An aggregation switch (MLAG pair) upgrade caused instability. During later vendor diagnostics, one switch's agent was killed, and its peer fell back to standalone mode, disrupting traffic. Pacemaker/DRBD fileserver pairs timed out, some STONITH commands never arrived, and in several pairs both nodes shut each other down. Fileserver recovery took more than five hours. GitHub now puts HA software into maintenance mode before network changes, stages switch upgrades first, and reduces cluster heartbeat dependence on the network. Tags: [partial failure, multi-step consistency] [topics: network partition, split brain, ha clusters, switches]. Source: https://github.blog/2012-12-26-downtime-last-saturday/
- **Discord, 2017-11: Cloud network upgrade causes repeated connectivity loss** — A Google Cloud network stack upgrade caused 16 brief connectivity losses on Discord's network-heavy sessions cluster. The Erlang net_kernel component then overloaded and wedged distribution ports, service discovery degraded, and the HTTP library misbehaved. Discord was fully offline twice for about 30 minutes, with many smaller interruptions across about 28 hours, until Google rolled back. Discord fixed the net_kernel overload, replaced its HTTP library and service discovery components, and improved P1 escalation with Google. Tags: [partial failure, overload] [topics: cloud networking, erlang, service discovery, cascading failure]. Source: https://discordstatus.com/incidents/mpzkz7m4fzpt
- **GitHub, 2018-10: 43-second partition forces cross-coast database failover** — Replacing failing optical equipment cut the US East hub off from the primary East Coast data center for 43 seconds. Orchestrator had quorum and promoted West Coast MySQL primaries, so both coasts ended up holding writes the other lacked. GitHub chose to fail forward and restore from backups, and service was degraded for 24h11m: 5M+ webhook events queued and about 200k payloads dropped past their TTL. GitHub stopped cross-region primary promotion and accelerated active/active/active serving and chaos testing. Tags: [partial failure, multi-step consistency, ordering] [topics: network partition, mysql, failover, replication]. Source: https://github.blog/2018-10-30-oct21-post-incident-analysis/
- **Google Cloud, 2019-06: Maintenance automation deschedules network control plane** — A maintenance event at one site triggered automation that, through a bug, descheduled network control-plane jobs across several locations at once. The network ran fail-static for a few minutes, then withdrew BGP routes between the affected sites. The result was heavy congestion and packet loss in six US/Americas regions for 3–4.5 hours, hitting GCE, Spanner, GCS and G Suite. Recovery was slow because the tooling was on the congested network. Google halted that automation, made the control plane reject such requests, persisted its configuration, and lengthened fail-static. Tags: [fan-out, partial failure] [topics: sdn control plane, bgp, maintenance automation, congestion]. Source: https://status.cloud.google.com/incident/cloud-networking/19009
- **Slack, 2021-01: Post-holiday surge overwhelms AWS Transit Gateway** — On the first workday of 2021, traffic with cold client caches outgrew how fast the AWS Transit Gateway scaled, and it began dropping packets. Packet loss saturated the web tier at the 7am peak. Autoscaling thrashed, and provision-service broke while adding about 1,200 servers, hitting open-file limits and AWS quotas. Slack's monitoring sat behind the same gateway. Slack was degraded or down for about 3.5 hours. AWS reviewed TGW scaling, Slack now requests pre-warming before holidays, and dashboards moved into the same VPC as their databases. Tags: [overload, partial failure] [topics: traffic spike, transit gateway, autoscaling, monitoring dependency]. Source: https://slack.engineering/slacks-outage-on-january-4th-2021/
- **AWS, 2021-12: Connection storm congests the internal network in us-east-1** — An automated scaling activity in the main AWS network triggered a surge of connections from internal-network clients. The surge overwhelmed the devices linking the two networks. Retries kept the congestion going, and a latent bug in client back-off stopped clients from backing off. Network devices took about 7 hours to recover, and some services took longer. Route 53 APIs, STS, the console and VPC endpoints were impaired, while running EC2 instances were not. AWS disabled the scaling activity, fixed the back-off bug, protected the devices, and rebuilt its Service Health Dashboard. Tags: [overload, fan-out] [topics: retries, backoff, network congestion, us-east-1]. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/message/12721/
- **Datadog, 2023-03: systemd security update deletes Cilium routes on every node** — An automatic systemd update arrived at 06:00 UTC through a legacy update channel. On Ubuntu 22.04, restarting systemd-networkd deleted the routes Cilium manages, and tens of thousands of nodes dropped off the network within an hour. The OS default update window hit five regions at the same time. Recovery and backfill took about two days. Datadog disabled the channel, configured networkd to leave foreign routes alone, audited for similar channels, and committed to clearer status and degraded-mode operation. Tags: [fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: kubernetes networking, cilium, systemd, os updates, multi-region]. Source: https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/2023-03-08-multiregion-infrastructure-connectivity-issue/
- **Heroku, 2025-06: Unplanned OS update strips routes from Private Space hosts** — An automated OS package update that should have been disabled ran on production and restarted the hosts' networking service. A legacy script applied routes only at first boot, so the routes never came back and dynos lost outbound connectivity. The status page and internal tools ran on the same infrastructure. Many customers were down for up to 24 hours. Heroku disabled the upgrade service, routed all system changes through controlled deploys, audited base images, and built independent communication channels. Tags: [fan-out, partial failure] [topics: os updates, routing, status page dependency, private networking]. Source: https://www.heroku.com/blog/summary-of-june-10-outage/

## CDNs and edge proxies

- **Cloudflare, 2017-02: Cloudbleed HTML parser leaks memory into responses** — A Ragel-generated HTML parser had an end-of-buffer check using `==` instead of `>=`. A new parser changed how buffers were passed and exposed the bug, so malformed tags made the old parser read past the buffer. Leaked memory (cookies, tokens, POST bodies) ended up in about 1 in 3.3M responses. 770 URLs on 161 domains were cached by search engines. Cloudflare had a mitigation in 47 minutes and a global fix within 7 hours, used feature kill switches, added bounds checks, fuzzed the code, and worked with search engines to purge caches. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: cdn, memory safety, parsing, caching, kill switch]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/incident-report-on-memory-leak-caused-by-cloudflare-parser-bug/
- **Cloudflare, 2019-07: Regex in a WAF rule pins every CPU** — A new WAF managed rule shipped in simulate mode, globally and with no staging. Its regex backtracked catastrophically, and HTTP-serving CPUs hit about 100% across the network. Visitors saw 502s and traffic dropped by about 80% for 27 minutes. A CPU-protection guard had been removed weeks earlier. Cloudflare restored the guard, reviewed all 3,868 rules, added performance profiling to tests, moved toward a regex engine with linear-time guarantees, and staged rule rollouts. Tags: [overload, fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: waf, regex, cdn, global config push]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/details-of-the-cloudflare-outage-on-july-2-2019/
- **Fastly, 2021-06: Customer config triggers latent bug across the CDN** — A bug shipped on 12 May lay dormant until a valid customer configuration change hit the exact conditions it needed on 8 June. About 85% of Fastly's network then returned errors. Fastly detected it within a minute, and 95% of the network recovered within 49 minutes once the configuration was disabled. A permanent fix followed the same day, and Fastly began examining why testing missed it. Tags: [fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: cdn, latent bug, configuration, blast radius]. Source: https://www.fastly.com/blog/summary-of-june-8-outage
- **Akamai, 2021-07: Configuration update breaks the edge DNS component** — A software configuration update triggered a bug in the DNS component of Akamai's Secure Edge CDN, so browsers could not be directed to many customers' sites. The disruption lasted up to an hour and ended when the update was rolled back. Akamai ruled out an attack, corrected its first statement on scope, and committed to reviewing its software update process. Tags: [fan-out] [topics: cdn, dns, configuration rollout, rollback]. Source: https://www.akamai.com/blog/news/akamai-summarizes-service-disruption-resolved
- **Canva, 2024-11: Stalled CDN asset releases a thundering herd** — After a deploy, a stale Cloudflare route sent IPv6 traffic over a lossy path with up to 66% packet loss. One JavaScript chunk stalled, and CDN request coalescing held about 270k users on the same fetch without erroring, so the canary didn't abort. When the chunk finally loaded, every waiting client hit the API at once, at about 1.5M req/s or 3x peak. Gateway tasks, already slowed by lock contention in a telemetry library, were killed for running out of memory faster than autoscaling could replace them. canva.com was down for about 52 minutes. Canva blocked traffic at the CDN and let it back in region by region under rate limits, added load shedding and asset fetch timeouts, and added page-load events as a canary signal. Tags: [overload, contention, fan-out] [topics: cdn, thundering herd, request coalescing, api gateway, load shedding]. Source: https://www.canva.dev/blog/engineering/canva-incident-report-api-gateway-outage/
- **Cloudflare, 2025-11: Duplicated query rows double the Bot Management feature file** — A ClickHouse permissions change made a metadata query return duplicate columns, so the Bot Management feature file roughly doubled in size and exceeded the proxy's hard limit of 200 features. The FL2 proxy panicked, and core CDN traffic returned 5xx errors from about 11:20 to 14:30 UTC, with full recovery at 17:06. Errors came and went because bad files came only from updated nodes, which at first looked like a DDoS. Cloudflare plans to validate its own generated config as strictly as user input, add global kill switches, and stop error reporting from consuming resources. Tags: [duplicates, trust but verify, fan-out] [topics: cdn, config propagation, clickhouse, kill switch]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage/
- **Cloudflare, 2025-12: Disabling a WAF test rule hits a nil bug in the old proxy** — While raising WAF body buffering to 1MB to protect customers from a React Server Components CVE, Cloudflare disabled an internal test rule through its global configuration system, which has no gradual rollout. In the older FL1 proxy, disabling a rule with an "execute" action hit a years-old nil-value bug. About 28% of HTTP traffic returned 500s for 25 minutes. Cloudflare froze network changes and prioritised versioned, staged config rollouts and fail-open error handling. Tags: [fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: cdn, waf, global config push, fail open]. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/5-december-2025-outage/

## Load balancers and proxies

- **AWS, 2012-12: Maintenance job deletes ELB state on Christmas Eve** — A developer with standing production access ran a maintenance process against live ELB state data and logically deleted part of it. Up to 6.8% of us-east-1 load balancers were affected: they could not be scaled or changed, and some returned errors. Recovery took about 24 hours. AWS now requires per-incident approval for that access, sped up recovery, and planned automatic reconciliation of ELB state. Tags: [trust but verify, multi-step consistency] [topics: load balancer, control plane, access control, data loss]. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/message/680587/
- **Slack, 2020-05: HAProxy runs out of backend slots and goes stale** — In the morning, a database problem triggered autoscaling that added about 75% more webapp instances, more than HAProxy's fixed per-AZ server slots could hold. The sync program tried to add new backends before freeing dead ones, so it kept failing, and HAProxy's backend list stopped updating. In the evening autoscaling removed the old instances HAProxy still pointed at, and users got 503s. The alert for this condition existed but was broken. A rolling HAProxy restart fixed it, and Slack planned a move to Envoy with xDS. Tags: [ordering, finding things fast, trust but verify] [topics: haproxy, consul, service discovery, autoscaling, load balancer]. Source: https://slack.engineering/a-terrible-horrible-no-good-very-bad-day-at-slack/
- **Discord, 2020-03: Truncated request writes empty service-discovery value** — A new API node's announce request to etcd was cut off after its headers. etcd's Go HTTP stack accepted it and wrote an empty value under a service-discovery key. Elixir watchers crashed parsing it, exceeded their supervisors' restart limits, and took down about half the watcher nodes. Discord was unavailable for most users for about 1h14m. Clients now send the whole announce in one packet, and watchers skip corrupt entries. Tags: [trust but verify, fan-out] [topics: service discovery, etcd, http, input validation]. Source: https://discordstatus.com/incidents/62gt9cgjwdgf
- **Google Cloud, 2021-11: Race in config pipeline makes global load balancer serve 404s** — A race condition added six months earlier to the pipeline that pushes customer configs to the external proxy load balancer corrupted a file after validation had already run. Most GCLB endpoints returned 404 for about 33 minutes, taking down Cloud Run, App Engine, Cloud Functions, Apigee and Firebase routes. Customer config changes were frozen for about 1h53m in total. Google rolled back to the last good config, closed the race, added input validation and pre-apply correctness checks, and added alerting. Tags: [ordering, trust but verify, fan-out] [topics: load balancer, configuration pipeline, race condition]. Source: https://status.cloud.google.com/incidents/6PM5mNd43NbMqjCZ5REh
- **Spotify, 2022-03: Traffic Director outage plus gRPC bug breaks service discovery** — A Google Cloud Traffic Director outage, combined with a bug in the gRPC client library, broke discovery for the services that used Traffic Director, and users worldwide were logged out and could not log back in. Services on Spotify's DNS-based discovery were unaffected. The outage lasted about 2h23m. Spotify moved affected services back to DNS discovery and added monitoring of discovery health. Tags: [finding things fast, partial failure] [topics: service discovery, grpc, xds, dns, managed control plane]. Source: https://engineering.atspotify.com/2022/03/incident-report-spotify-outage-on-march-8/
- **HubSpot, 2024-09: Uneven Envoy pods cascade at the morning peak** — HubSpot's Envoy routing pods were unevenly spread across Kubernetes nodes. During a morning traffic spike, many pods on the overloaded nodes failed readiness at the same time and were pulled from service, pushing more load onto the rest until the routing layer collapsed. A fix deployed the day before had silently not taken effect. Two outages totalled about 56 minutes. HubSpot rolled the routing layer back to Nginx, fixed pod spread, limited how many pods load balancers can eject under load, and now validates pod distribution before deploys. Tags: [hot spots, overload] [topics: envoy, kubernetes, readiness probes, load balancer, cascading failure]. Source: https://product.hubspot.com/blog/hubspot-incident-sept25

## Traffic spikes, DDoS and rate limiting

- **Stripe, 2017: Four layers of rate limiting and load shedding (engineering write-up)** — Stripe describes its production limiters. A per-user request rate limiter (token bucket in Redis) rejected millions of requests a month, mostly runaway test scripts. A concurrent-request limiter fired about 12k times a month. A fleet load shedder reserves capacity for critical calls such as charges, and a worker-utilisation shedder drops low-priority traffic by tier during incidents. Practices include failing open if the limiter breaks, returning 429 vs 503 correctly, dark-launching limiters, and keeping kill switches. Tags: [overload, contention] [topics: rate limiting, token bucket, load shedding, redis]. Source: https://stripe.com/blog/rate-limiters
- **GitHub, 2018-02: 1.35 Tbps memcached amplification attack** — Attackers spoofed GitHub's address against internet-exposed memcached servers over UDP, amplifying traffic up to about 51,000x. The peak was 1.35 Tbps and 126.9M packets/s, from over a thousand ASes. GitHub was down from 17:21 to 17:26 UTC and intermittently unavailable until 17:30, after it withdrew transit announcements to route through Akamai's scrubbing, then applied ACLs. GitHub planned to automate mitigation activation from its monitoring and to expand transit and edge capacity. Tags: [overload] [topics: ddos, amplification, udp, bgp, scrubbing]. Source: https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/ddos-incident-report/
- **Healthchecks.io, 2025-11: Tarpit rate limiting exhausts load-balancer connections** — A misconfigured client pinged one check about 300 times a second. HAProxy's tarpit rate limiting held each rejected connection open for a second, which used up connection slots instead of protecting them. Pulling one load balancer from DNS rotation shifted its load to the others and made things worse. The service was intermittently unavailable for about 1h40m. The fix: block the source, replace tarpit with immediate 429s, add request timeouts, raise maxconn, and alert on session counts. Tags: [overload, hot spots] [topics: rate limiting, haproxy, connection limits, dns rotation]. Source: https://blog.healthchecks.io/2025/11/post-mortem-outage-on-november-1/
- **Google Cloud, 2025-06: Null policy field crash-loops Service Control, then a retry herd** — A quota-check feature shipped on 29 May without a feature flag or error handling. On 12 June, a policy row with blank fields replicated globally within seconds and crashed Service Control in every region, causing API 503s across Google Cloud and Workspace. Restarting tasks without randomised backoff then overloaded the us-central1 Spanner table, so most regions recovered within 2 hours while us-central1 took up to about 2h40m to resolve fully (the status dashboard shows the incident open from 10:51 to 18:18 PT). Google is making Service Control fail open, rolling out global data incrementally, flag-gating new code, and auditing backoff. Tags: [overload, fan-out, trust but verify] [topics: api gateway, retries, backoff, feature flags, global replication]. Source: https://status.cloud.google.com/incidents/ow5i3PPK96RduMcb1SsW

## TLS and certificates

- **Mozilla, 2019-05: Expired intermediate certificate disables Firefox add-ons** — The intermediate certificate that signed every add-on's certificate expired just after 01:00 UTC on 4 May. Because Firefox re-checks signatures about every 24 hours, most of the 15,000+ add-ons were gradually disabled for users worldwide. Mozilla shipped a replacement intermediate with the same subject and key through its Studies system about 9 hours after work began, then shipped dot releases for users the Studies system couldn't reach. Mozilla's follow-up committed to a non-Telemetry fast-update path, better documentation, and QA coverage of certificate expiry. Tags: [trust but verify, fan-out] [topics: tls, certificate expiry, code signing, pki]. Source: https://hacks.mozilla.org/2019/05/technical-details-on-the-recent-firefox-add-on-outage/
- **PyPI, 2021-04: Internal certificates expire and the CRL overflows on recovery** — One-year internal TLS certificates for Vault expired, so the deployment tooling could no longer start pods or read secrets. Redeploying Vault to reissue them failed: a backlog of revoked 30-day certificates made the certificate revocation list larger than the storage backend allowed, and Vault crashed every time it became leader. Uploads and uncached requests failed for about 2 hours. The fallback mirror lacked `requires-python` metadata, so some users got the wrong versions. PyPI added alerts at 75% of certificate lifetime and retuned revocation. Tags: [trust but verify, multi-step consistency] [topics: tls, certificate expiry, vault, crl, fallback]. Source: https://status.python.org/incidents/rw171ylf8jw3

## Added by lesson upgrades (append-only)

- **Datadog, 2021-09: Aggressive gRPC reconnect setting floods VPC conntrack on every rollout** — Starting in September 2021, rollouts of the metrics query service caused errors that first looked like DNS (clients kept stale pod IPs for 30–60 seconds). The root cause was a custom, very aggressive gRPC reconnect setting: about 900 clients retried a deleted pod's IP roughly every 300 ms, up to 30,000 SYNs per second, and one old IP received about 90,000 connection attempts in 90 seconds. The SYNs filled the AWS VPC connection-tracking table, and reverse path filtering dropped them silently after Cilium removed the pod's route. Removing the custom reconnect parameters (back to gRPC defaults) stopped the floods; Datadog also raised node-local-dns memory from 64 MB to 256 MB and contributed a Cilium change that adds an unreachable route for old pod IPs. Tags: [retry storm, finding things fast] [topics: grpc, reconnect backoff, kubernetes, conntrack, dns, cilium]. Source: https://datadoghq.com/blog/engineering/grpc-dns-and-load-balancing-incident

- **Valve Steam, 2015-12: Caching configuration serves logged-in store pages to other users** — On 25 December, during a DoS attack that pushed Steam Store traffic 2,000% above typical sale levels, a second caching configuration was deployed that wrongly cached web traffic for authenticated users. Between about 11:50 and 13:20 PST, store page responses for about 34,000 users may have been shown to others, including billing addresses, purchase history, the last two digits of card numbers, the last four digits of Steam Guard phone numbers and email addresses; full card numbers and passwords were not exposed. Valve shut the store, deployed a new caching configuration, confirmed edge caches were purged before reopening, and said it would contact affected users. Tags: [trust but verify, overload] [topics: cdn, caching, personalised responses, ddos, data exposure]. Source: https://store.steampowered.com/oldnews/19852

- **GitHub, 2022-10: retries of impossible webhook jobs stall delivery** — On 5 October, automated accounts rapidly created and deleted repositories. Many webhook jobs could not build their payloads because the data was already deleted; the delivery workers kept retrying them, which blocked new events for 31 minutes (06:30 to 07:01 UTC) and turned GitHub Actions red. GitHub disabled the accounts, then changed workers to skip retries for jobs whose data no longer exists. Tags: [overload, partial failure] [topics: webhooks, retries, poison messages, queues]. Source: https://github.blog/2022-11-02-github-availability-report-october-2022/
- **GitHub, 2024-07: config change strips auth from webhook background jobs** — On 5 July, 16:31 to 18:08 UTC, a configuration change removed authentication from the background-job requests webhooks rely on, so they were rejected and external deliveries failed; delays averaged 24 minutes, up to 71. A secondary crash loop in the job API layer followed until 21:14 UTC. GitHub repaired the config, improved health checks and alerts, and is working on workload isolation. Tags: [partial failure] [topics: webhooks, config change, background jobs]. Source: https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-availability-report-july-2024/
- **Plane, 2026-10: webhook SSRF fix bypassed by DNS rebinding (CVE-2026-104973)** — An earlier fix validated webhook IP addresses only when the webhook was created; the delivery task re-resolved DNS on each send without checking the result, so an admin controlling DNS could reach internal services. CVSS 3.1 score 7.6, fixed in 1.4.0. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: webhooks, SSRF, DNS rebinding, TOCTOU]. Source: https://app.opencve.io/cve/CVE-2026-104973
- **Shopify, 2022-11: BFCM Live Map moves from WebSocket plus polling to SSE (engineering write-up)** — The 2021 map pushed data over WebSocket into a mailbox that browsers polled at least every 10 seconds, and trending products could lag by minutes. The 2022 rebuild used a Go SSE server reading Kafka behind nginx, JWTs in an Authorization header via an EventSource polyfill, and a custom load generator; data reached clients within milliseconds of availability and within 21 seconds end to end, with 100% uptime. Tags: [fan-out] [topics: server-sent events, websockets, polling, kafka]. Source: https://shopify.engineering/server-sent-events-data-streaming
- **LinkedIn, 2016-10: 100K SSE connections per node for instant messaging (engineering write-up)** — LinkedIn chose SSE over WebSockets for network compatibility. Load tests hit a 128-entry accept backlog (somaxconn), a 30,000 file-descriptor limit, a thread leak from one HashedWheelTimer per connection, and about 20 KB of JVM heap per TLS connection; after raising limits and heap to 16 GB, each node held about 100,000 persistent connections. Tags: [overload] [topics: server-sent events, persistent connections, file descriptors, JVM]. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/archive/instant-messaging-at-linkedin-scaling-to-hundreds-of-thousands-
- **Google Chrome, 2019: WebRTC host candidates switch from private IPs to mDNS names** — Sites had been harvesting private IPs through WebRTC, likely for tracking. Chrome replaced host-candidate IPs with random .local mDNS hostnames, at 50% of users and then fully in Chrome 76 (August 2019), exempting pages with camera or microphone permission. Services that parsed candidates as IPs failed or fell back to less direct paths. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: webrtc, ICE, privacy, NAT traversal]. Source: https://groups.google.com/g/discuss-webrtc/c/6stQXi72BEU/m/2FwZd24UAQAJ
- **Discord, 2018-09: WebRTC at 2.6 million concurrent voice users (engineering write-up)** — 850+ voice servers in 13 regions pushed over 220 Gbps and 120 Mpps. Clients always connect to a Discord relay, so native apps drop ICE, use Salsa20 instead of DTLS/SRTP and a ~1,000-byte join message instead of SDP; a custom C++ SFU bridges browser and native clients and hides user IPs. Tags: [fan-out] [topics: webrtc, SFU, voice, UDP]. Source: https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-handles-two-and-half-million-concurrent-voice-users-using-webrtc

## Added during the sd-003 lesson upgrade (TLS and load balancing)

- **Let's Encrypt, 2021-09: DST Root CA X3 cross-sign expires** — On 30 September 2021 the old root that cross-signed Let's Encrypt expired. Devices without ISRG Root X1 in their trust store began failing, and OpenSSL 1.0.x clients failed even when they trusted ISRG Root X1, due to a chain-building quirk. Older Android kept working only via a special cross-sign valid past the root's expiry. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: tls, root certificates, certificate expiry, openssl]. Source: https://letsencrypt.org/docs/dst-root-ca-x3-expiration-september-2021/
- **Dropbox, 2019-09: Server-reported load replaces local in-flight counts in Bandaid (engineering write-up)** — Bandaid used random two choices over each proxy's local active-request counts, but some servers still hit full utilisation at peak. Backends began returning utilisation in an X-Bandaid-Utilization response header; proxies score candidates on it, with time decay and a penalty for recent 5xx so fast-failing servers do not attract traffic. Tags: [hot spots, overload] [topics: load balancing algorithms, power of two choices, backend load reporting]. Source: https://dropbox.tech/infrastructure/enhancing-bandaid-load-balancing-at-dropbox-by-leveraging-real-time-backend-server-load-information
- **Dropbox, 2024-10: Robinhood PID-controlled weights (engineering write-up)** — A per-node PID controller steers each backend toward average utilisation and ships weights to clients over Envoy EDS for weighted round robin. Max/avg CPU fell from 1.26 to 1.01 on an Envoy cluster and from 1.4 to 1.05 on a database frontend; some large services cut fleet size by 25%. Tags: [hot spots] [topics: load balancing, weighted round robin, xds, capacity]. Source: https://dropbox.tech/infrastructure/robinhood-in-house-load-balancing-service
- **Uber, 2022-05: Real-time dynamic subsetting (engineering write-up)** — Static, random fixed-size subsets in on-host proxies concentrated load on some backends. Subset size now grows with each proxy's share of traffic, using load aggregated by the control plane. Of 36 large services onboarded last, 17 cut p99 CPU utilisation by 10% or more, some by up to 40%. Tags: [hot spots, fan-out] [topics: subsetting, service mesh, load balancing]. Source: https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/better-load-balancing-real-time-dynamic-subsetting/
- **Vimeo, 2016-12: Bounded-load consistent hashing in HAProxy (engineering write-up)** — Vimeo's Andrew Rodland implemented Google's "Consistent Hashing with Bounded Loads" (Aug 2016) in HAProxy for Vimeo's video load balancing, now the hash-balance-factor option (0 = off; 125–200 suggested). Google reports it cut Vimeo's cache bandwidth by a factor of almost 8 and removed a scaling bottleneck. Tags: [hot spots] [topics: consistent hashing, bounded loads, haproxy, caching]. Source: https://research.google/blog/consistent-hashing-with-bounded-loads/
- **GitHub, 2018-08: GLB Director uses rendezvous hashing for L4 load balancing (engineering write-up)** — GLB keeps a forwarding table of about 65k rows (about 512 KB), each with a primary and secondary proxy chosen by rendezvous hashing of server IP and row number. Adding a server moves about 1/N of flows; draining swaps primary and secondary so the draining proxy forwards unknown flows to the other; only one proxy may be draining or filling at a time. Tags: [partial failure] [topics: rendezvous hashing, load balancing, connection draining]. Source: https://github.blog/engineering/infrastructure/glb-director-open-source-load-balancer/
- **Linux, 2012-06: Leap second livelocks hrtimer/futex users** — After the 30 June 2012 leap second, many Linux systems showed futex load spikes in MySQL, Java, Firefox and Thunderbird. The timekeeping code inserted the second without calling clock_was_set(), so CLOCK_REALTIME hrtimers fired a second early and sub-second timeouts fired immediately in a loop. Workaround: set the clock to itself (date -s "$(date)"). John Stultz's patch series (5 July 2012, sent to stable) called clock_was_set() on leap-second changes and made hrtimer_interrupt refresh base offsets. Tags: [trust but verify, overload] [topics: time, leap second, linux kernel, hrtimer, futex]. Source: https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/1207.0/02318.html

## HTTP/2 abuse (appended by sd-026)

- **Google Cloud, 2023-08/10: HTTP/2 Rapid Reset (CVE-2023-44487)** — Attackers sent HEADERS and an immediate RST_STREAM in a loop, so they never exceeded the concurrent-stream limit while servers kept starting work; the largest attack Google saw exceeded 398 million requests per second (peaks in August, disclosed 11 October 2023). Recommended mitigations: close the whole connection on abuse, send GOAWAY that stops stream creation immediately, flag connections with many cancelled requests, and close connections that exceed the stream limit. Tags: [overload, trust but verify] [topics: http2, ddos, rst_stream, goaway]. Source: https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/how-it-works-the-novel-http2-rapid-reset-ddos-attack

## Crawlers and the sites they hit (appended during the sdi-042 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

- **Read the Docs, 2024-05/06: AI crawlers re-download the same files** — One crawler pulled 73 TB of zipped HTML in May 2024, nearly 10 TB in one day, costing over $5,000 in bandwidth; a bug made it download the same files repeatedly, it had no bandwidth limit, and it did not use ETag or Last-Modified. - **Wikimedia, 2025-04: scrapers miss the edge cache** — Multimedia bandwidth demand grew about 50% from January 2024, mostly from automated scraping of Wikimedia Commons. Bots were about 35% of pageviews but at least 65% of the most resource-consuming traffic, because bulk crawling reads unpopular pages that miss regional caches and go to the core data center. When Jimmy Carter died, a 1.5-hour debate video doubled network traffic and saturated some links for about an hour. Tags: [overload, hot spots] [topics: web crawler, cdn, cache miss, capacity]. ## Video delivery (appended during the sdi-039 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

## Time zone rule changes (appended during the sdi-049 lesson upgrade, October 2026)

- **Egypt, 2016-07: DST cancelled two days before it started** — tzdata carried Egypt's planned DST start on 7 July 2016; the government reversed on short notice, and tz release 2016f (5 July 2016) removed it. Any system that had already converted future local times to UTC under the old rule was an hour off until it re-derived them. Tags: [trust but verify] [topics: tzdata, daylight saving, calendars]. Source: https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/NEWS
- **Lebanon, 2023-03: three tz releases in a week** — 2023a (22 March) kept Lebanon's normal DST start; 2023b (23 March) moved it to 20/21 April after a government announcement; 2023c (28 March) reverted to 2023a's data after the decision was reversed. Systems that re-indexed on 2023b and not again on 2023c were wrong for a month. Tags: [trust but verify, multi-step consistency] [topics: tzdata, daylight saving, calendars, re-indexing]. Source: https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/NEWS
